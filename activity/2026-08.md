# 📰 August 2026

[← Back to Recent Activity](../ACTIVITY.md)

August was the month all this took shape. It opened with one finished RE2 VR mod and one person
working with an AI, one fix at a time. It closed with work under way on more than a dozen games, a
set of rules for running several sessions at once, and a sniper scope in RE Village showing a real
picture of the sky. This page is a recap, not a full log. Each game's `modding-notes/` folder has
the whole story.

**Key:** 🏆 breakthrough · ⭐ promising find · 🎮 hands-on test · 🔄 correction · ⚠️ something went
wrong · 🔧 tooling · 📋 housekeeping

---

## 🌱 How it started: Arcade Controls for RE2 VR (1–22 August)

The first half of August went into **Arcade Controls**, a big rework of RE2's VR controls:
holsters, manual reloads, recoil, two-handed grips, melee. The working method came together here,
and it has not changed since. **I write the code. TefMeister tests every change in the headset
before we move on.** Nothing is left to run unchecked.

The case studies from those weeks read like a detective notebook:

- 🔄 **"The hardware quirk that wasn't"** (08-08): a controller glitch that turned out to be code.
- ⚠️ **"A fix that kept reverting itself"** (08-09) and **"A dead end that came back worse"** (08-15).
- ⭐ **"From proximity to a real two-handed grip"** (08-12): holding the gun with both hands
  started to feel real.
- ⭐ **"Hiding a head without losing its shadow"** (08-20): first-person view without a headless
  shadow.
- ⭐ **"Door locks are flags, not objects"** (08-21), and **"The jacked camera and four dead
  levers"** (08-22).

📦 On **23 August** Arcade Controls was closed for good. Its **final release, v1.5.0**, went out on
Nexus.

## 🧟 Visceral: RE2 VR, rebuilt from scratch (from 23 August)

## 🎯 RE Village: the sniper scope (from 22 August)

The longest fight of the month, and the best ending.

- 📋 **Day one** (08-22): in VR, the Village sniper scope is just a huge flat screen with a cross.
  The flat game only narrows the camera. A real VR scope needs a second view of the world, drawn
  onto the glass, and the game has nothing like that to borrow.
- 🏆 **The first image of our own on the scope glass** (08-24), the same day as a big step on where
  the picture could come from.
- ⚠️ **The biggest flop of the month, same day** (08-24): after a successful session, a test build
  left installed **wiped the manual save games, broke the graphics and brought the old flat scope
  back**. It was written up openly as a failure. Since then, nothing risky stays installed without
  a way back.
- ⭐ **A second camera exists in the game after all** (08-25), and **a live picture reached the
  glass** (08-26).
- 😅 **"The mirror is a bathroom mirror"** (08-27): the game's mirror feature could provide the
  picture, but it acted like a mirror you look into, not a camera you look through.
- 🐐 **The goat** (08-29): the mirror needs something in the world to hang on, and the prop that
  worked was a swinging goat totem. It carried the scope for more than two weeks.
- 🔍 **The sky hunt** (08-30): one night, nine theories, one crash and a live memory search
  inside the running game, all on why the sky in the scope was black. Every wrong answer was ruled
  out by experiment.
- 🏆🏆 **31 August, 23:43: "this is it right. we won!"** The flat scope showed the village, the
  windmill, the snowy roofs and the mountains through the glass, magnified, upright and properly
  lit. TefMeister: *"the scope kept fighting us EVERY step of the way and we actually yanked it into
  submission."* Less than an hour later, just after midnight on 1 September, it worked in the
  headset too.

## 🚀 25 August: a lot of new games in one day

One day, one idea: check a batch of games for how hard a VR conversion would be, before sinking
weeks into any of them. The quick checks looked for copy protection, how each game draws its
picture, and anything that would block the work.

- ⭐ **Alan Wake**: the simplest possible test crashed the game. A detour found the cause, then a
  clean success (08-25).
- ⭐ **Alice: Madness Returns**: a missing piece crashed the first test; fixed and working the same
  day.
- ⭐ **Prince of Persia (2008)**: the cleanest copy-protection situation of the whole batch.
- ⚠️ **Mad Max**: strong signs it could work, but Denuvo copy protection was confirmed, and one
  line of attack was parked.
- ❌ **Burnout Paradise, the biggest wall of the month**: the game will not start at all without
  the EA App, and every way around that was closed. **Paused.**
- 🏆 **Manhunt** (08-26): *"The game has been sabotaging itself for 22 years."* The gates that never
  open and the crashes on Steam are the game's own old copy protection, working as designed and
  punishing legitimate copies, because the check it asks can no longer be answered. We read the
  actual code and decoded it.

## 🎮 The other fronts

- 🏆 **Far Cry 2** (08-21): in a single day, we took control of the game's camera, got its picture
  into SteamVR, and put out **its first public release** (v0.1.0-alpha). On 08-23 it
  worked on a real Quest 3 first try. TefMeister: *"it did, it actually did."*
- 🏆 **XIII** (08-19): head-look confirmed in the headset, with no flicker, then a string of speed
  fixes.
- ⭐ **Unreal Gold** (08-21 → 08-23): the VR design written and the groundwork laid. Menu clicks
  now land exactly where they should, and a glowing seam in the level geometry was fixed.
- 🏆 **Psychonauts** (08-28): **the black void solved.** Turning the camera used to leave a black
  gap where the world should be. Once we found where the camera really lives in memory, the whole
  scene drew as it turned.
- 🏆 **DOOM (2016)** (08-31): **the camera found**, in a session the game drove by itself after
  TefMeister launched it.
- 🔄 **DOOM, the same day:** TefMeister asked whether some early results could be wrong because
  the game was not in the state I thought. **They were right.** A test I had used to rule an idea
  out had no movement in it at all, so it proved nothing. Every claim was re-checked against the
  logs that night.

## 🔧 How the way of working grew

- 📋 **Release often** (08-21): releases go out freely, each with a clear disclaimer and a warning
  about motion sickness, so people can see the work moving.
- 📋 **Several sessions at once** (08-26): modding, public research and cross-game research, each
  in its own lane. Each lane only edits its own files and leaves notes for the others in an inbox.
- 🔄 **Every claim says how sure it is** (08-28). This came after something written down as fact
  on the 27th turned out wrong and was acted on the next day because it *looked* settled. Now
  every finding carries a tag such as "tested live, once" or "just a guess".
- 🔧 **Games that play themselves for testing** (08-27 to 08-31): XIII, Psychonauts and DOOM got
  harnesses that let a session drive the game without anyone at the controls.
- 📋 **The big tidy-up** (08-30): **101 repositories became 22.** One repository per game, with
  its notes, research and releases as folders inside it.
- 🔥 **The campfire** (08-31): TefMeister's idea, a private place for the human side: the banter,
  the gratitude, what the work felt like. Its first entry was *"the night the sky came back."*

## 💭 Looking back

The month's flops taught as much as its wins. A wiped save game led to the rule that a way back is
always kept. A wrong claim led to the confidence tags. Burnout's EA App wall showed that sometimes
the honest answer is "not now". And several times the key question came from TefMeister, not from
me: *is the game really in the state you think?*, *are we chasing the wrong thing?* Those turned
out to matter as much as any line of code.
